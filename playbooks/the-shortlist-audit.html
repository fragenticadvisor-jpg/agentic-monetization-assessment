Why this playbook exists
Buyers increasingly point an agent at the research phase before a human ever opens a comparison tab: it reads vendor sites, pulls case studies, and builds a shortlist the human then reviews. A homepage built to be skimmed by a person and a homepage built to be parsed and cited by a system are different documents, and most companies have only ever built the first one. This playbook tests whether your go to market is legible to the reader that increasingly decides who makes the list.

Approach 1
Human First GTM
Content written to be skimmed by a person, top to bottom

Five signals your GTM was built for a reader that no longer does the first pass

Mark each one honestly against your current site and sales content.

✓
Your homepage's core claim can't be extracted as a single, quotable paragraph
A research agent summarising you will pull whatever is structured clearly enough to lift: if that's not your real differentiation, it will surface the generic version instead.
✓
You've never tested how an AI tool describes your company in its own words
Buyers increasingly let an agent build the first pass shortlist. If you don't know what it says about you, you don't know if you're on that list.
✓
Your case studies read as narrative, not as citable facts with named outcomes
A system extracting proof points needs numbers and named results, not a story: narrative only case studies rarely make it into a summarised comparison.
✓
Content is written assuming a human reads it top to bottom, not a system parsing for facts
The two documents look similar to a person but behave very differently to a system doing extraction: most sites have only ever optimised for the former.
✓
Nobody in the company owns the question of how you look to an agent doing vendor research
This is a new and specific responsibility. Without an owner, it never gets tested, and problems are discovered only when a deal that should have shortlisted you never does.

The 30 day action plan
Week 1
Test your own visibility. Ask two or three AI research tools to describe your company and your differentiation, and compare the answers to what you'd actually say.
Week 2
Rewrite your core claim as one quotable paragraph. State what you do differently in plain, factual language a system could lift directly, not narrative.
Week 3
Restructure two or three case studies as citable facts. Lead with the named outcome and the number, not the story arc.
Week 4
Assign an owner. Someone needs to re test agent visibility on a regular cadence as both the tools and your positioning evolve.
