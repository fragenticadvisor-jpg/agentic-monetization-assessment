Why this playbook exists
The job of the seller changes the moment the product itself starts doing work a person used to do. A demo that shows features solves the old problem — will this do what I need — and ignores the new one a buyer is actually asking: why pay a premium for this when an agent could plausibly do it for less. Sales enablement built around feature walkthroughs has no answer to that question, and reps default to discounting because they were never given one.
Two ways sales teams respond to the AI question, compared

Only one of these holds price under real buyer scrutiny.

Approach 1
Feature Recitation
Demo walks through capabilities, one screen at a time
Easy to train, easy to script
Feels thorough — covers everything the product does
Never actually answers why pay for this if the AI does the work
Buyers who are already comparing to a cheaper alternative aren't moved
Approach 2
Outcome Selling
Conversation anchored on the outcome and its value, not the feature list
Directly answers the question buyers are actually asking
Gives reps a real position instead of a discount as the only lever
Requires reps to know the quantified outcome value, which takes real preparation
Harder to script than a feature walkthrough, so it needs coaching to land consistently
Reality check
Discount as Default
No real answer, so price becomes the only lever left
Closes the deal in the room
Trains buyers to wait for the discount every cycle
Erodes the pricing model the rest of the business is trying to build
Why-Pay Reality Check
Two questions to find out whether your sales team can hold price when a buyer asks the AI question directly.
If a buyer asked your rep today why pay for this instead of doing it with a general AI tool, could they answer without a discount?
Is your sales training and comp built around outcome selling, or around feature demos?

Five signals your sales motion has no answer to the AI question

Mark each one honestly against your last quarter of deals.

✓
Discounting is the default response when a buyer compares you to a general AI tool
A discount is what a sales motion falls back on when it has no real answer to why pay for this — it treats the symptom, not the actual objection.
✓
Sales training still centres on a feature-by-feature demo
Feature demos answer will this do what I need, not why pay a premium for it — the second question is the one buyers are now actually asking.
✓
Reps can't state the outcome value of a deal in a number, only the feature list
Outcome selling requires a quantified answer. Without one, a rep facing the AI-comparison objection has nothing to hold price with.
✓
Comp still rewards demo completion and activity, not outcome-framed deal quality
What gets measured gets practiced — if comp doesn't reward outcome selling, reps won't build the muscle for it under pressure.
✓
No one has rehearsed the specific objection: why not just use an AI tool for this
This is now a predictable, common objection. An unrehearsed rep improvises a defensive answer, usually a discount.

The 30-day action plan
Week 1
Pull the deals where the AI-comparison objection came up. Look at what happened next — did the deal discount, stall, or close at full value, and why.
Week 2
Draft the real answer. Write a quantified outcome-value statement for your top two use cases that a rep can actually say out loud.
Week 3
Rehearse it. Run the objection live in a call-review or role-play session until reps can deliver the answer without reaching for a discount.
Week 4
Align comp to reinforce it. Adjust what gets recognised in deal reviews so outcome-framed selling is the visibly rewarded behaviour.
