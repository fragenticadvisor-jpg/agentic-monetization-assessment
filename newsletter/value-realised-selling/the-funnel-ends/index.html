The Funnel Ends. The Customer Doesn’t.

Eight stages. Your funnel covers five. The revenue sits in the other three, and your process stopped before it got there.

The Agentic Commercial Model · Fessal Rahman · November 4, 2026 · 7 min read

All EpisodesSeason 1Season 2Season 3Value-Realised SellingSubscribe on LinkedIn

The funnel ends. The customer does not.

That single mismatch is why most sales methodologies are about to be exposed as a description of the seller's activity rather than the buyer's, and why the forecasts built on them will keep lying for another two or three quarters before anyone admits why.

A funnel is a shape that narrows and stops. It was the correct shape for a business where revenue locked at signature. You sold two hundred licences, you booked two hundred licences, and whatever happened afterwards was somebody else's department. Attrition was the only interesting variable, which is exactly what a funnel is designed to describe.

That business is going away.

Under consumption and outcome pricing, the signature is not the revenue. It is permission to start earning it. The money arrives afterwards, in proportion to what the customer actually does with the thing you sold them.

Which makes the handoff the most expensive artefact in your commercial model, because it happens precisely where the earning begins.

The shape that replaces it

The customer notices a constraint. They frame it internally and build a case. They compare options, increasingly with an agent doing the comparing. They test. They commit. They deploy. They realise value, or they quietly fail to. And the success creates the next constraint, which starts the whole thing again.

Eight stages. Your funnel covers five of them. The revenue sits in the other three.

It has no end, so neither can the selling.

Why this is a commercial model problem, not a sales problem

There is a comfortable reading of all this, which is that sellers need to be more consultative, build deeper relationships, and stay close after the close. Every sales conference has been saying a version of that for thirty years and it has changed almost nothing, because it asks individuals to compensate for a structure.

The structure is the problem. If the compensation plan pays on contract value, nobody will work the last three stages. If the forecast is built on closed won, nobody will measure them. If customer success reports to a different leader with a different number, the handoff is not a cultural failure, it is an org chart working exactly as designed.

So this series is not about technique. It is about what has to change in the commercial operating model for the last three stages to have an owner, a metric and a reason to exist.

What AI actually changed

Two things, and only one of them gets discussed.

The first is that discovery has been commoditised. A model can research a prospect's constraints faster and more thoroughly than any account executive, and increasingly the buyer runs that research themselves before anyone picks up a phone. Finding pain is no longer a differentiator. It is a free input.

The second is that the buyer now arrives with an agent. The shortlist is assembled by something that reads your documentation, your pricing page and your reviews, and never takes your call. Most go to market machinery is still built to perform for a human who is no longer the first reader.

What has not been commoditised is quantifying the constraint in the customer's own numbers, and then designing a commercial structure that gets paid when the constraint actually goes away. That is the job now. It is a harder job than the one it replaced, and almost nobody is hiring for it.

What this series will cover

Nine parts, running the whole loop, in three acts.

Demand. Notices, Frames, Compares. Where the deal is shaped before you know it exists, and what you can actually influence from outside the room.

Decision. Tests, Commits. Where the existing process still works, including the hardest objection in enterprise software right now, which is why a customer should buy seats for work your product does without them.

Realisation. Deploys, Realises, Expands. Where the revenue is, where the handoff sits, and why measurement stops being reporting and becomes the invoice.

Each part names what changes in the commercial operating model to make that stage work, because without that the advice is just encouragement.

The position

Selling did not get harder. What you are paid for moved, and almost nobody moved the process to follow it.

A funnel told you when you were finished.

Nothing in this one does.
