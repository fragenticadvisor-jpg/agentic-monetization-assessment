The Half-Pivot

Why Agentic Failures Get Blamed on Execution

The post-mortem always blames execution. It is almost never execution. There was no commercial model to execute.

Part of the Agentic Commercial Model essay series · Fessal Rahman

When an agentic AI initiative underperforms, the post-mortem almost always lands on execution: the rollout was too slow, the sales team wasn't enabled properly, adoption lagged. That diagnosis is usually wrong, or at least incomplete, because it assumes there was a coherent commercial model to execute against in the first place. In most of the cases worth studying, there wasn't.

The tell is in how these initiatives get announced: with a launch date, a keynote, a press release — the packaging of a product launch, not the packaging of an operating-model rebuild. Pricing, sales compensation, customer success structure, and the metrics used to judge success are all supposed to move together for a transformation like this to hold up, and in practice they rarely do. New language gets layered onto old architecture instead.

The pattern is visible in public pricing history. One CRM vendor changed its flagship agent pricing model three separate times within eighteen months — moving between per-conversation, credit-based, and seat-bundled structures — which is less a sign of iteration and more a sign that nobody had settled what the product fundamentally was. A major support-software vendor added AI resolution fees directly on top of its existing per-seat pricing, producing a bill customers found difficult to predict or defend internally, rather than replacing the old unit with a coherent new one.

The gap that does the most damage is usually invisible from the outside: sales compensation still tied to bookings and seat expansion, even as the company's public narrative is entirely about agentic outcomes. That leaves sales reps rationally optimizing against what actually shows up in their paycheck, not the strategy in the deck. Companies still running a comp plan built for the previous era while claiming a fully agentic future are handing a structural advantage to whichever competitor actually finishes the rebuild.
