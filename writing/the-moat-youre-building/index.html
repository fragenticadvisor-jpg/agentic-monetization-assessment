The Moat You're Building Without Realising It

Every customer you onboard today is training your model on exactly how they work. That calibration doesn't transfer.

Part of the Agentic Commercial Model essay series · Fessal Rahman

Competitive moats in software have historically decayed. A feature advantage gets copied, a distribution advantage gets matched, and the gap closes over time. Agentic AI introduces a different kind of advantage, one that behaves the opposite way: it can widen the longer a vendor holds the relationship, because the system is continuously learning the specific, idiosyncratic way each customer actually works.

The distinction that matters is between two kinds of learning. Across-user learning is the industry-wide improvement that benefits every customer roughly equally — a model gets generally smarter, and every account benefits a little. Within-user learning is different: it's the account-specific calibration to one customer's workflows, edge cases, and operational quirks, and it is close to worthless to a competitor trying to win that account away, because it doesn't transfer with the contract.

The pattern is visible across categories that have been at this the longest — coding assistants that improve through millions of accumulated developer interactions, data platforms whose connector libraries deepen with every integration a customer builds, and security products whose detection quality compounds with every signal a specific customer's environment has fed into it over time. None of that history is available to a new entrant on day one, no matter how good their underlying model is.

The practical implication is that early revenue is not the real prize — usage depth is. A company sitting on a smaller but deeply calibrated customer base may be harder to dislodge than one with more logos and shallower usage. That argues for treating usage depth as a defensibility metric in its own right, prioritizing speed of deployment to start the calibration clock sooner rather than later, and making the accumulated, customer-specific advantage visible to the customer directly at renewal — because if the customer doesn't know what they'd lose by switching, the moat isn't doing its job.

In This Issue

  • Why agentic AI moats compound over time instead of decaying, unlike most historical software advantages.
  • The distinction between across-user learning (shared, transferable) and within-user learning (account-specific, sticky).
  • Examples of categories where within-user calibration has already become the real competitive asset.
  • Why usage depth — not early revenue — should be the metric leadership tracks as a defensibility signal.
