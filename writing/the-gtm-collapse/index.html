The GTM Collapse

The go-to-market motion built for human buyers is collapsing under the weight of agentic AI.

Part of the Agentic Commercial Model essay series · Fessal Rahman

A large and fast-growing share of software buyers now start vendor research inside an AI assistant rather than a search engine or a company website. By the time a prospect fills out a form or takes a call, an AI system has often already narrowed the field to a handful of vendors — frequently well under ten — based on sources the vendor never controlled and, in many cases, never optimized for.

That creates a three-stage collapse in the traditional funnel. First, the shortlist gets built invisibly: AI models generate a small set of credible vendors before any human contact occurs, using signals a marketing team can't see happening in real time. Second, a huge amount of go-to-market spend keeps flowing into channels built for buyers who never show up — SEO-tuned landing pages, retargeting campaigns, and nurture sequences performing, in effect, for an empty room. Third, when AI does evaluate a vendor, it leans on third-party signals — analyst coverage, earned media, independent reviews — rather than the company's own polished messaging, and generic SEO copy tends to lose to specific, authoritative, third-party-verifiable content.

The implication is not that marketing stops mattering — it's that the terrain marketing needs to win on has moved. Owned-channel optimization for a human funnel is fighting the wrong battle when the decisive shortlisting increasingly happens somewhere the vendor doesn't have direct control over.

Rebuilding around this reality means measuring a different thing: not top-of-funnel visits, but whether the company actually shows up inside AI-generated answers; not just pipeline volume, but authority in the third-party venues those answers draw from; and a sales motion redesigned for buyers who arrive already informed and can move to a decision in days rather than quarters, because the traditional education stage of the funnel has already happened somewhere else.

In This Issue

  • The scale of AI-assisted vendor research now happening before a human ever contacts a vendor directly.
  • The three-stage collapse: invisible shortlisting, funnels optimized for buyers who never arrive, and AI's preference for third-party sources over owned content.
  • Why authority in analyst reports, earned media, and reviews now matters more than owned-channel SEO.
  • What a go-to-market motion built for pre-informed buyers actually needs to measure and do differently.
