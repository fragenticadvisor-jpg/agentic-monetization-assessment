About the Book

Dead Model Walking

Why Agentic AI Breaks the Business of Software, and the Model That Wins

Every major business is racing to deploy AI. Almost none of them have asked what happens to their commercial model when it works. The pricing models, compensation plans, and revenue metrics that built SaaS were designed for a world where humans were the buyers, the users, and the expanders. AI agents are replacing all three.

Dead Model Walking makes the case that most commercial leaders are not transforming their model — they are relabelling it. Salesforce changed Agentforce pricing three times in eighteen months. Zendesk charges per resolution on top of per-seat fees, not instead of them. First-mover advantage in the agentic era is now close to permanent.

Book Two

Dead Gods Walking

How Agentic AI Builds Around Business Leaders Who Won't Adapt

The sequel to Dead Model Walking. Where the first book killed the commercial model, this one is about the people still defending it. It reimagines Charles Handy's Gods of Management as Greek archetypes — Cronus, Zeus, Sisyphus, Narcissus, and Midas — none of whom survive the agentic era.

The central argument: leaders who will not adapt are not fired. They are built around. Still in the building, still on the org chart, no longer load-bearing. Grounded in named, real-world cases, from Intel to Salesforce to Broadcom's acquisition of VMware.
