Agentic Layer Check

Notion

From today, every existing Notion Business and Enterprise workspace runs on a new credit system, and it draws a line nobody else in this series has drawn. One allowance for work where Notion picks the model. A second, smaller one for work where you pick it. The meter now prices a choice, not just an amount. However, neither allowance pools, transfers or carries over, so a team's unused capacity disappears every month while its heaviest users sit blocked. Here's how it reads against the nine layers.

Agentic Layer Check · Public signal read · November 2026

What they sell

A connected workspace for notes, documents, wikis, projects and databases, now with an assistant, meeting notes, enterprise search and agents that act on the same workspace content.

Who they sell to

Individuals and small teams self serve on Free and Plus, growing companies on Business, and larger organisations on Enterprise, where the agents, search and admin controls live.

How they price

Seats first: Plus $10 and Business $20 per member a month, Enterprise custom. Then two monthly allowances on every Business and Enterprise seat, 1,500 standard credits and 500 select credits, with extra workspace credits sold at $10 per 1,000 monthly. New workspaces moved on 19 October 2026, existing ones on 2 November 2026.

What's working

Two decisions here are better than anything else this series has found, and both run against the way the rest of the market is moving.

Layer 6 · Metering & Entitlement

The meter splits on who picks the model

Every Business and Enterprise seat carries two separate monthly balances. Standard credits cover the work where Notion chooses the model behind the scenes: the assistant on auto, editing, translation, formulas and meeting notes. Select credits, at a third of the size, cover the work where the customer chooses the model or runs an agent they built themselves. The same question answered two ways costs differently depending on who made the decision. Every other meter in this series counts volume, effort or outcomes. This one prices control, and it lines the cheap path up with the path the vendor can optimise, without forbidding the expensive one.

Layer 3 · Pricing & Packaging

The saving went back to the customer, not the margin

Notion says its new routing delivers comparable results at roughly 90% lower average cost across more than twenty uses, and that agent runs came in around 30% cheaper in testing. It could have kept that. Instead the seat price held and the allowances arrived on top, while the agents themselves went from a five minute ceiling on a single task to over twenty four hours. The job got three hundred times longer and the customer's bill did not move. That is the right answer to falling delivery costs: improve what the same money buys rather than cutting the price, because a price cut is permanent and a capability increase compounds.

Where there's room to extend it

The allowances are generous. The rules about who owns them are where the value leaks.

Layer 8 · Revenue Architecture · Opportunity

Every balance is personal, and none of it carries

Per Notion's own documentation, the two allowances reset monthly, unused credits don't roll over, and the balances cannot be pooled, transferred between members, or converted from one type into the other. Picture a team of ten where two people do nearly all the agent work. Eight people's allowances expire untouched at the end of the month while those two sit blocked, and no amount of unused capacity elsewhere in the workspace can reach them. The workspace paid for all of it. The design then sells the answer separately, as pooled workspace credits at $10 per 1,000. Letting the per seat allowances pool, or carry for a month or two, would turn the most common usage pattern in any team from a monthly write off into a reason to expand.

Layer 7 · Adoption & Customer Success · Opportunity

Running out puts a person in the loop

When an allowance is spent, the features that draw on it pause until the next monthly reset. Continuing on workspace credits is possible, but it is switched off by default and has to be turned on by an admin, and a member who hits the limit is invited to request more credits from that admin. So the most engaged users in the building are the ones who get stopped, and the way forward runs through an internal approval rather than a self serve top up. Nothing about that is unreasonable, and the spend visibility it gives admins is genuinely useful. However, the first experience of the new system, for the people who use it most, is an interruption and a favour to ask.

How this moves through the model

Notion's own framing is that most people are unlikely to run out, and that is where the two findings meet. An allowance sized so it rarely binds is not really a meter, it is a seat with a usage shaped label, which lands on Pricing & Packaging (Layer 3): the revenue still comes from seats, and the credits mostly exist to cap the heaviest users and to sell a pooled top up to the teams those users work in. It lands on Sales Strategy & Enablement (Layer 4) too, because three currencies now sit on one screen, personal standard, personal select, and pooled purchased, each with its own rules about who can spend it and when it disappears, and a rep has to explain which one a customer is actually about to run out of. Notion has built the most thoughtful meter in this series and attached it to balances that nobody can share. The pricing decision is sound. The ownership rules behind it are what will decide whether the usage grows.
